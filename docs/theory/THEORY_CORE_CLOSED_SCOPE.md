These are the load-bearing pieces a first citation should start from.

## Scaffolding And Ledgers

Files with names such as:

```text
StatusLanding
ResidualSplit
PublicWrapper
Capstone
AuditBody
ObligationStatus
```

are usually route ledgers, audit surfaces, wrappers, or open-slot records.  They
are useful for keeping obligations visible, but they are not the main evidence
for the theory and should not be counted as independent mathematical depth.

If a wrapper claims that something is `discharged`, inspect what it discharges:

- a real theorem body or finite bound can be cited;
- a status equality, unresolved marker, or certificate-relative projection is
  bookkeeping unless backed by a source theorem listed above.

## Downstream Readout Routes

G2/G3/G4 readout routes organize capacity, exponent, and policy-body claims
inside the interface.  They are certificate-relative unless a native source
theorem is supplied.

Safe reading:

```text
G2/G3/G4 record where native-domain certificates would enter.
They do not currently prove native capacity, full LDP, effective-bandwidth,
Shannon, or general policy theorems.
```

`unresolved` entries are open slots in the meta-theory.  They are useful
guards, not mathematical closures.

## Outside The Current Core

The current core does not claim:

- native capacity, native exponent, or measure-theoretic LDP theorems;
- Shannon-style converse or achievability theorems;
- automatic adapter discovery for arbitrary hidden-state systems;
- empirical truth, forecasts, population generalization, or sampling theory;
- globally standardized numerical units for all persistence/collapse claims;
- free transfer between unrelated domains;
- AI shutdown, jailbreak, alignment, or LLM-performance guarantees;
- software-collapse prediction without domain evidence.

These are not failures of the core.  They are native-domain or empirical
responsibilities that must be supplied separately when a concrete readout needs
them.
